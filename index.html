Communicator, teacher, and catastrophe scientist.

I trained as a catastrophe scientist: tsunami hazard on Caribbean subduction zones, volcanic-eruption mortality across 18th-century Britain. Since then I have spent fifteen years making hard things legible, as a photojournalist published across Europe, an evidence-based advocate in animal welfare, and a teacher of English to everyone from medical students to airline pilots. Now I build small, working models that put those threads together, and I am moving the combination, hazard thinking plus plain communication, into work on global catastrophic risk, where the hardest tail events are the ones without a base rate.

Reduced-form model, assumption-audited

Nuclear winter explorer

An interactive, dependency-free model of how a nuclear or volcanic winter cools the planet and stresses food production. The major uncertainties, soot load, lofting, aerosol lifetime, food sensitivity, are exposed as sliders rather than buried in prose, and the model audits its own assumptions. It shows conditional pathways, not forecasts; I removed the earlier mortality estimates once they implied more precision than the science supports.
